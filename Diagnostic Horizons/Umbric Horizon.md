#Horizons 

## General Description

An umbric horizon (from Latin umbra, shade) is a relatively thick, dark-coloured surface horizon with a low base saturation and a moderate to high content of organic matter.

## Diagnostic Criteria

An umbric horizon is a surface horizon consisting of [[Mineral Material|mineral material]] *and* has:
1. single or in combination, in ≥ 50% (by volume):
a. soil aggregate structure with an average aggregate size of ≤ 10 cm; *or*
b. cloddy structure or other structural elements created by agricultural practices;
*and*
2. ≥ 0.6% [[Soil Organic Carbon|soil organic carbon]];
*and*
3. one *or* both of the following:
a. in ≥ 90% of the exposed area of the entire horizon or of the subhorizons below any plough layer, a Munsell colour value of ≤ 3 moist, *and* ≤ 5 dry, *and* a chroma of ≤ 3 moist;
*or*
b. all of the following:
i. a texture class of loamy sand or coarser; *and*
ii. in ≥ 90% of the exposed area of the entire horizon or of the subhorizons below any plough layer, a Munsell colour value of ≤ 5 *and* a chroma of ≤ 3, both moist; *and*
iii. ≥ 2.5% soil organic carbon;
*and*
4. if a layer is present that corresponds to the parent material of the umbric horizon and that has a Munsell colour value of ≤ 4, moist, ≥ 0.6% (absolute) more soil organic carbon than this layer;
*and*
5. a base saturation (by 1 M NH4OAc, pH 7) of < 50% on a weighted average;
*and*
6. a thickness of one of the following:
a. ≥ 10 cm if directly overlying [[Continuous Rock|continuous rock]], [[Technic Hard Material|technic hard material]] or a [[Cryic Horizon|cryic]], [[Petroduric Horizon|petroduric]] or [[Petroplinthic Horizon|petroplinthic]] horizon; *or*
b. ≥ 20 cm.

## Field Identification

The main field characteristics of an umbric horizon are its dark colour and its structure. In general, umbric horizons tend to have a lesser grade of soil structure than mollic horizons.

Most umbric horizons have an acid reaction (pHwater < 5.5), which usually indicates a base saturation of < 50%. An additional indication for strong acidity is a shallow, horizontal rooting pattern in the absence of a physical barrier.

## Relationships with other Diagnostics

The base saturation requirement sets the umbric horizon apart from the [[Mollic Horizon|mollic horizon]], which is otherwise similar. The upper limit of the content of soil organic carbon is 20%, which is the lower limit for [[Organic Material|organic material]].

Some [[Irragric Horizon|irragric]] and [[Plaggic Horizon|plaggic]] horizons may also qualify as umbric horizons.
